Recently, Rev. Amitha Khema shared a Dhamma discourse on the Four Foundations of Mindfulness (*Satipaṭṭhāna*) at the International Buddhist Association of America monthly gathering at the Thai Buddhist Temple in Sterling, Virginia.

The Buddha taught these four foundations as a direct path toward inner purification, wisdom, peace, and liberation. They ask us to observe experience carefully, without clinging and without pushing away.

1. **Mindfulness of the body** (*kāyānupassanā*): breath, posture, movement, and the physical nature of the body.
2. **Mindfulness of feelings** (*vedanānupassanā*): pleasant, unpleasant, and neither.
3. **Mindfulness of the mind** (*cittānupassanā*): the mind as it is in this moment.
4. **Mindfulness of mental phenomena** (*dhammānupassanā*): the patterns that shape what we take to be a self.

Mindfulness is not limited to a formal sit. The Buddha encouraged awareness while walking, speaking, eating, working, and being with other people.
